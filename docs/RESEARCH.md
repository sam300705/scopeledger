# ScopeLedger: product selection memo

Research date: 14 September 2026. Desk research, not customer interviews.

## Decision

Build a focused scope-change register for small service companies. Start with Indian web/software agencies and implementation consultancies with roughly 5–30 delivery staff. Expand only after repeated use and paid retention.

A product for "most companies" is too broad for a first sale. This product fits firms delivering fixed-scope client work. It is not aimed at retailers, every enterprise department, or firms already happy with an integrated professional-services suite.

## Problem and supporting evidence

Unpriced additions can consume delivery capacity without a matching commercial agreement. Screendragon describes uncontrolled change as extra scope without formal rebalancing of time, cost or resources, and recommends a visible change-control process. It also offers intake, planning, change logs and reporting itself.

Source: https://www.screendragon.com/blog/scope-creep-meaning-and-how-to-avoid/

Atlassian defines scope creep as expansion beyond the original plan and discusses its effects on project cost, time and resources. This supports the existence of the problem, not demand for this particular product.

Source: https://www.atlassian.com/work-management/project-management/scope-creep

Projectworks discusses unbilled, favor-based work in professional services. This is a particularly relevant buyer context.

Source: https://www.projectworks.com/blog/scope-creep

## Competitor map

| Alternative | Evidence from product material | Positioning hypothesis for ScopeLedger |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Screendragon | Broad intake, resource planning, workflow, proofing, change logs, reporting | A narrower adoption path for small firms that do not need a full operations platform. |
| ManyRequests | Agency client portal and request-management platform. Its pricing page advertises unlimited clients, an included seat and additional-seat pricing. | Sell a focused internal commercial record that works beside an existing task tool. Do not claim lower total cost without comparing actual buyer needs. |
| Projectworks | Professional-services context, including project profitability and unbilled work | Focus on the decision before work starts rather than replacing professional-services management. |
| Spreadsheet + email | An obvious, readily available substitute rather than a sourced market statistic | Role checks, linked evidence, consistent estimates, and an application activity log must justify switching. |

No evidence establishes low competition, a defensible moat, a specific market size, or guaranteed sales. Name/trademark availability has not been checked; ScopeLedger is a working name.

## Proposed differentiator

A five-minute baseline setup, an extra-work estimate at the saved rate, a copyable client proposal, evidence-backed staff recording of decisions, and exports. Price and adoption simplicity are hypotheses. This release makes no AI extraction or automatic scope-classification claims.

## Customer discovery: validate before expanding

Ask 10 agency owners or delivery leads about their most recent completed fixed-price project:
1. What was requested after the agreed scope? Ask for a concrete example.
2. How did you decide whether it was included?
3. How many extra hours were worked, quoted, approved, invoiced, and actually paid? Keep these separate.
4. Where is the approval today, and how often do you need to find it?
5. What process/tool do you already use, and why would you change it?
6. Who can authorize spending on a tool, and what must it replace or save?

Demonstrate one real workflow using sanitized records. Do not collect unnecessary client documents. Seek three company pilots and payment from at least two before building costly integrations. These are proposed decision gates, not research findings.

## Explicit kill/reposition conditions

Reposition if most interviewed firms do not record change requests, do not experience material unpaid additions, or find their existing spreadsheet adequate. Do not treat compliments, free signups, or the size of a generic project-management market as validation.
