# ScopeLedger: first sales playbook

## Buyer and pitch

First buyer: owner or delivery lead of a 5–30-person software/web agency or implementation consultancy doing fixed-scope client projects.

Pitch: "When clients ask for extra work, your team needs a clear fee and a written decision before starting. ScopeLedger keeps the original scope, extra-work estimate and approval evidence together, so nothing gets lost between a client email and delivery."

Do not promise guaranteed margin recovery, automatic legal enforcement, electronic signatures, revenue collection, no competitors, or fully automated scope detection.

## Offer to test — not an established market price

- 30-day assisted pilot: suggested ₹1,999 for one company workspace.
- Optional onboarding service: proposed ₹3,000–₹5,000 after confirming the amount of setup work.
- After demonstrated value: test ₹2,999/month with the buyer. Do not publish discounts or subscriptions as live until delivery and billing arrangements are real.

These prices are experiments, not forecasts or verified competitor comparisons. The app does not process payments or enforce subscriptions. Any commercial pilot currently needs an agreed manual invoicing arrangement and an accessible deployment.

## Six-minute demo

1. Create a company workspace or use the clearly labelled fictional sample.
2. Open one project and read its original exclusions.
3. Log an extra request: e.g. 12 hours at ₹2,500/hour = ₹30,000 excluding taxes.
4. Show the proposed fee and schedule impact; copy the client proposal.
5. Explain that the client responds through their normal channel. Record approval only after receiving it.
6. Demonstrate that an editor cannot approve, show the activity history, and export the change ledger.

End with: "Can we run this on one active project for 30 days and measure whether it helps your team quote and agree on extra work?"

## Prospect selection

Start with agency owners who openly sell fixed-price website, software, ERP, CRM or design implementation projects. Use public business information and manual research. A good prospect has several concurrent client projects and an owner-led purchasing decision.

Do not mass-message people. No outreach has been sent and no prospect list or customer agreement has been fabricated.

## Draft outreach (review before sending)

"Hi [Name], I’m building a small tool for agencies to track extra client requests and get the cost agreed before the team starts. It keeps the original scope, estimate and approval evidence together. Does unpaid scope creep come up in your projects? If yes, I’d value 15 minutes to understand your process and show you what I’ve built."

## Measure the pilot

Record starting process, active projects, extras captured, quoted value, approved value, hours spent chasing decisions, time to decision, and weekly use. Separately verify invoiced and paid amounts in the company’s billing system. App approval totals alone are not proof of financial ROI.

## Before a real company can use it

- The delivered Site is owner-private. Configure an appropriate audience before inviting a company; workspace roles alone do not change Site access.
- The current authentication path is ChatGPT sign-in. Confirm this is acceptable to the buyer; implement an independently hosted, reviewed identity path if it is not.
- Agree the service scope, privacy/data handling terms, retention/deletion and support expectations. Avoid claiming certifications.
- Complete real browser acceptance checks with the buyer’s sign-in path and test the company workflow on production-like infrastructure.
- Establish a backup-and-restore procedure and operational monitoring for the paid deployment. The included CSV exports are not a full database restore mechanism.

## Objection handling

"We use spreadsheets." — Ask where evidence and authorization live, then demonstrate the role restrictions and history. If a spreadsheet works well, do not force the sale.

"Can it send reminders or bill customers?" — Not in this release. It produces proposal text and exports; it does not send or collect anything automatically.

"Is client approval legally signed?" — No. Staff record the existing written decision. It is not an electronic-signature service.
